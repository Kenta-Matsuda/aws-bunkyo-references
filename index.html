They were found by following links from official pages, opening the listings of other AWS blogs, searching with results limited to a single official domain, and opening the listing data of official sites. Links were followed from the education and research industry pages, program pages, event pages, blog posts that introduce resources or events, and the 128 education-related posts on the Japanese blog.
  • Listing data is what the case study search page, the event materials page, the blogs, What's New, the Solutions Library, and the event listings load after opening (aws.amazon.com/api/dirs/items/search). It can be fetched directly and filtered by industry or keyword. Of the 331 entries added in the follow-up survey on October 4, 2026, 285 came from this listing data.
  • Treated as official: AWS and Amazon sites (aws.amazon.com, docs.aws.amazon.com, repost.aws, builder.aws.com, awsstatic.com, pages.awscloud.com, resources.awscloud.com, aboutamazon.com, amazon.science, and so on), AWS GitHub organizations, and YouTube videos. YouTube is limited to videos linked from official pages or official listing data, and videos whose search-result titles include the name of an AWS event (re:Invent, AWS Imagine). For the latter, the publishing channel was not verified.
  • Not included: generic pages whose connection to education or research could not be confirmed; redirected pages whose original content is no longer visible, and 404 pages; individual AWS Marketplace product pages and partner directory pages (written by sellers or partners); Amazon-wide social-impact articles that do not mention AWS; AWS's own new-graduate recruiting and internship notices; and articles about re-employment support for working adults (AWS re/Start).
  • Titles and URLs are as of the time of retrieval (October 3–4, 2026). Dates are the publication or event dates shown on the page or in the listing data. Year-only dates are cases where the listing data has only a year, or where the listing date was a placeholder that did not match the event date. This index is an unofficial list built from AWS's public information.

Verification status of other official resources

  • Opened and checked (905) – the page was opened and its content checked.
  • Linked from an official page (223) – a link appears on an opened official page, post, or listing page, or in the official listing data. The linked page itself was not opened (this includes YouTube, video pages, PDFs, and other pages that cannot be opened automatically).
  • Seen in search results (128) – appeared in search results limited to official domains. The content could not be opened because fetching is prohibited or refused, or only a generic page is returned. Known reasons are given in the note.
  • Checked in a browser (4) – fetching was refused and the page does not appear in search, so a person opened it in a browser (the re:Post Education Excellence Hub and the 3 articles listed there).
  • Of the entries added in the October 4 follow-up survey, 52 were sampled and re-opened by a separate reviewer not involved in the work. No errors were found in titles, URLs, or verification status. For the kind whose notes had included details not found in the materials (event session slides), all 78 opened entries were compared with the materials and 40 notes were corrected to what the materials actually say. 5 entries whose main topic could not be called education were removed.

What may be missing

  • Only two listings can be said to be fully included: the Education category of the English Public Sector Blog and the Public Sector category of the Japanese blog. In the listing data, everything was reviewed for English case studies in the education industry (225), event materials in the education industry (101), and the Education category of all blogs (1,228 of 1,238); of these, entries whose main topic is education and which can still be opened are included. Everything else is what searching turned up.
  • Other AWS blogs – posts with the Education category were cross-checked using the listing data for all blogs (the 10 that could not be read were judged, from their dates, to be already-included Public Sector Blog posts). Posts without the category were chosen by title from each blog's listing. The full post listing was read to the end for 19 blogs (HPC, Desktop and Application Streaming, Training and Certification, Open Source, Quantum Technologies, and others). Machine Learning, AWS News, and APN were read up to page 115 from the newest side; Security, Database, Architecture, Containers, Media, Big Data, and Storage only through category and tag listings; Startups and Game Tech only partially. DevOps, Developer Tools, Industries, Microsoft Workloads, SAP, .NET, Supply Chain, Cloud Financial Management, Integration & Automation, and IBM & Red Hat were not opened. Posts whose titles contain no education or research words and that lack the Education category cannot be picked up.
  • The Japanese blog-wide listing – posts from March 8 to June 23, 2024 were seen only in the General and Case Study category listings (97 posts). There are also 6 places where one page (10 posts) could not be opened (February 2018, September 2020, late July 2022, early September 2023, June 2025, April 2026), and a gap in early November 2025. Keyword searches of the listing data filled in some of this, but search only matches titles and summaries, so posts that mention a school only in the body cannot be picked up. Of the 258 results for 学生 (student), 80 (posts from May 2024 to February 2025) could not be read because fetching was refused.
  • Posts not in any listing – the English Public Sector Blog had one post that appeared in neither the category listing nor the blog-wide listing (a Moodle how-to; it is included on the resources side). Listing pages can return slightly different results on each fetch, so there may be other such posts.
  • Sites where fetching is prohibited – pages.awscloud.com and resources.awscloud.com (webinar recordings, registration pages, PDFs), awseducate.com, and YouTube were verified only through official-page links, listing data, or search results. pages.awscloud.com has many registration pages for past webinars in various countries, and each search turns up pages that are not included.
  • Pages that load content after opening – the case study search page, event materials, the Solutions Library, What's New, and event listings were viewed through the underlying listing data. However, the case study listing data stops at June 2025 and the event materials listing data at re:Invent 2024; anything newer is only what appeared in search. The workshop catalog, the archive of Japanese event materials (aws.amazon.com/jp/events/aws-event-resource/archive/), and the AWS Summit Japan session catalog could not be viewed because the listing data name is unknown or it could not be fetched.
  • Pages that no longer exist – many older case studies, partner case studies, AWS EdStart pages, and the like now redirect to listing pages or other pages. Of the 310 English education case studies in the listing data, 192 redirected to the general case studies page or returned 404. In Japanese, the case study pages for Fujita Health University, KEK, and Casio redirect (PDF versions of the latter two are included). URLs confirmed to redirect are not included (they are recorded in the working documents).
  • AWS Summit Japan materials – each year from 2016 to 2026 was searched with 86 searches limited to official domains, and through the Japanese blog listing data. Only a few new items were found, such as session slides from the Institute of Medical Science, the University of Tokyo (2018) and Life is Tech! (2021). For the 2018 case study sessions, the storage location's sequential numbers 01–46 were opened in order; of the 39 that existed, only 1 could be confirmed as a session by an educational or research institution (5 could not be read). Most materials from 2019 onward (pages.awscloud.com) and the session listings (awsevents.com) could not be opened and rarely appear in search. The RIKEN "Virtual Fugaku" session (2024, CUS-38) was confirmed from a blog post, but no URL for its materials or video on an official domain has been found.
  • Sessions at re:Invent and other events – in the event materials listing data (2019–2024), all 101 entries in the education industry and all search results for 18 terms such as education, university, and student were reviewed, and 87 sessions whose main topic is education were added (slides and videos are separate rows). About 70% of entries tagged with the education industry were not about education, and conversely the education sessions at re:Invent 2020–2022 had no industry tag. re:Invent 2025 is not in the listing data; only what appeared in search is included. Sessions without published slides cannot be picked up.
  • re:Post and AWS Builder Center – re:Post Selections (curated topic pages) shows 99 entries in its listing, but page fetching could read the titles of only 12; individual pages cannot be opened and do not appear in search. The only one included is the Education Excellence Hub, checked in a browser. re:Post questions come only from search-result titles, and only some AWS Builder Center articles could be read. Both include posts by members, so the poster is given in the note.
  • Education pages for individual services – across 40 searches and link-following from official pages, only 1 new product page explicitly for education was found. The AppStream 2.0 education page, the Alexa education page, AWS IoT EduKit, the Japanese DeepRacer student league page, and others now redirect to generic pages.
  • GitHub, session slides, and partner case studies – only what search found and what was linked from official pages. What's New was searched in the listing data with about 20 terms.
  • Number of page fetches – many consecutive fetches are refused for about 15–25 minutes (HTTP 429). Refused pages were not retried. For this reason, 7 of the entries added on October 4 could not be opened and are marked "Linked from an official page".
  • Number of searches – searches are limited to 200 per work session. The additional survey on October 4, 2026 hit this limit, leaving the searches for Japanese case studies, AWS Summit Japan materials by year, education sessions at re:Invent 2021–2023, and education pages for individual AWS services. A follow-up survey the same day covered these 4 with 185 searches and the listing data. What was and was not found is described in the items above.
